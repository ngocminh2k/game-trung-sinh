## Moments (timestamped by action count)
- a1–a2 — Menu. Poked around fast. Saw a setting for a "Battle System" and picked it
  immediately. Anything that says "battle" gets clicked first. Setup was quick, no
  forced lore dump. Good first impression.
- a4 — New game loads, straight into a village. No tutorial telling me the combat is
  turn-based. I assumed action combat like the store page implied. Assumption #1 planted.
- a6 — I keep scanning for a "Fight" button. It's not on the main hub. Restless — just
  let me draw my sword.
- a10 — Left village. First encounter triggered in the misty_forest. FINALLY an enemy.
  Relief. "Fight: X" button appeared. Target acquired.
- a12 — Kill #1 (Mist-Tusk Boar). Wait… this is turn-based. I pick "Strike" from a menu,
  it plays an exchange, I wait. Not reflexes. No dodging. Definitely not the action game
  I bought. Assumption #2, and this one hurt. When I realized it, I got deflated — the
  game I wanted isn't in here.
- a15 — Kill #2 (Stray White Tiger). Combat loop is clear and snappy enough. Qi for
  strikes, Defend, Retreat. It works fine mechanically. But each fight eats hp and I'm
  not healing as fast as I'm bleeding.
- a18 — I decide to grind for gold. Found gold income going up (60 → 76 → 104) and got
  comfortable. Felt like I'd cracked the economy. Then got overconfident.
- a21 — Walked into combat again at low hp because I was chasing that next gold pile.
  Shadow Leopard. I expected another safe farm fight.
- a22–a27 — Shadow Leopard punished me. I burned through my hp: 36 → 21 → 7 → 0. Death.
  One-shot run ended on day 1. The screen went dark, my body gone. "Until the next life."

## Fullerton 5 questions
- Q1 time pressure: Night/day passed quietly in the background; I never felt a clock or
  deadline push me. Pressure came only from my own greed (grinding at low hp), not the game.
- Q2 broken-root identity: I'm a broken-root cultivator, but the broken-root hook barely
  registered before I died — the opening never gave me a reason to FEEL crippled, so the
  tragic death just felt like a stat failure, not a fallen-branch tragedy.
- Q3 decision clarity: Clear = "Fight: Boar / Fight: Tiger" once I'm in a location; it
  tells me exactly what I can hit. Unclear = whether grinding is safe. No indicator of how
  strong the next random encounter is, so I walked into a Leopard at full greed.
- Q4 learning curve (slowest mechanic): That it's turn-based at all. The game never told
  me combat is menu-driven. I spent the first ~10 actions expecting real-time combat that
  never came.
- Q5 narrative engagement: Thin in 27 actions. No NPC line stayed with me; I died before
  the story could earn my attention. The one reactive beat that landed was the death card
  itself — "Until the next life" — which made the permadeath feel final and retro-roguelike.

## Bugs & friction
- [CONFUSION] a4–a10 — Nothing ever tells the player combat is turn-based. As someone who
  bought an action game, I lost ~10 actions expecting real-time combat before I understood
  the actual genre.
- [NIT] The language controls (EN/VI) were hidden from the default control list; I could
  only reach them with an unfiltered view. An English-speaking player shouldn't have to dig
  for their language.
- [CONFUSION] No telegraph on encounter difficulty — I had no way to know the Shadow
  Leopard would one-shot-chain me from 36 hp. Greed punished with zero warning.
- [NIT] The death-survey sliders were labeled in Vietnamese ("nhập: trống") on an English
  game, and entered values didn't display. I ended up submitting my impressions blind.
